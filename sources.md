# Sources

The frozen bibliography. Each entry in the form it takes in the paper's `## References`, with a one-line provenance note. Every in-text author-year resolves here and back (`papers refs`). Verified July 2026 via publisher / NBER / JSTOR / RePEc / DOI records.

## Bibliography

Allen, R. C. (1983). Collective invention. *Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization*, 4(1), 1–24. — RePEc jeborg v4y1983i1p1-24, DOI 10.1016/0167-2681(83)90023-9; supports foundational work as collectively produced (shared blast-furnace designs), weakening single-inventor capture claims.

Arrow, K. J. (1962). Economic welfare and the allocation of resources for invention. In *The rate and direction of inventive activity: Economic and social factors* (pp. 609–626). Princeton University Press. — RePEc nberch/2144; the non-appropriability of information as a public good. NOTE: 1962 volume has no named editor; do not add one.

Bresnahan, T. F., & Trajtenberg, M. (1995). General purpose technologies: 'Engines of growth'? *Journal of Econometrics*, 65(1), 83–108. — DOI 10.1016/0304-4076(94)01598-T; GPTs, innovational complementarities.

Cerf, V. G., & Kahn, R. E. (1974). A protocol for packet network intercommunication. *IEEE Transactions on Communications*, 22(5), 637–648. — Confirmed COM-22(5), May 1974; the openly published TCP design, no per-packet royalty. Anchors the packet-toll thought experiment.

Christophers, B. (2020). *Rentier capitalism: Who owns the economy, and who pays for it?* Verso. — Verified Verso 2020; rents from control of scarce assets rather than production.

Cournot, A. A. (1897). *Researches into the mathematical principles of the theory of wealth* (N. T. Bacon, Trans.). Macmillan. (Original work published 1838) — Ch. IX, complementary monopoly (copper + zinc into brass); the serial-toll result the model reproduces.

David, P. A. (1985). Clio and the economics of QWERTY. *American Economic Review*, 75(2), 332–337. — RePEc aecrev v75y1985i2p332-37; path dependence / lock-in via network effects and switching costs.

Giblin, R., & Doctorow, C. (2022). *Chokepoint capitalism: How big tech and big content captured creative labor markets and how we'll win them back.* Beacon Press. — Verified Beacon Press 2022; names the chokepoint mechanism the paper formalizes.

Heller, M. A., & Eisenberg, R. S. (1998). Can patents deter innovation? The anticommons in biomedical research. *Science*, 280(5364), 698–701. — DOI 10.1126/science.280.5364.698, PMID 9563938; the tragedy of the anticommons.

Hoffmann, M., Nagle, F., & Zhou, Y. (2024). *The value of open source software* (Harvard Business School Working Paper No. 24-038). Harvard Business School. — HBS 24-038 (Jan 2024); ~$4.15B supply-side cost vs ~$8.8T modeled demand-side replacement value. Demand-side is a modeled counterfactual; attribute as estimated.

Krueger, A. O. (1974). The political economy of the rent-seeking society. *American Economic Review*, 64(3), 291–303. — RePEc aecrev v64y1974i3p291-303; coined "rent-seeking."

Lemley, M. A., & Shapiro, C. (2007). Patent holdup and royalty stacking. *Texas Law Review*, 85, 1991–2049. — Stanford Law / Haas PDF; the named "royalty stacking" phenomenon (magnitude contested in later literature).

Mazzucato, M. (2013). *The entrepreneurial state: Debunking public vs. private sector myths.* Anthem Press. — Verified Anthem 2013; the state as foundational risk-absorber.

Mazzucato, M. (2018). *The value of everything: Making and taking in the global economy.* Allen Lane. — Verified Allen Lane 2018; value extraction narrated as value creation.

Nordhaus, W. D. (2004). *Schumpeterian profits in the American economy: Theory and measurement* (NBER Working Paper No. 10433). National Bureau of Economic Research. — nber.org/papers/w10433; innovators capture about 2.2 percent of the social surplus (nonfarm business, 1948–2001), reported in body and NBER Digest (abstract says "minuscule fraction"). LOAD-BEARING.

Romer, P. M. (1990). Endogenous technological change. *Journal of Political Economy*, 98(5, Pt. 2), S71–S102. — DOI 10.1086/261725; nonrivalry of ideas.

Samuelson, P. A. (1954). The pure theory of public expenditure. *Review of Economics and Statistics*, 36(4), 387–389. — JSTOR; founding statement of nonrival public goods.

Shapiro, C. (2001). Navigating the patent thicket: Cross licenses, patent pools, and standard setting. In A. B. Jaffe, J. Lerner, & S. Stern (Eds.), *Innovation policy and the economy* (Vol. 1, pp. 119–150). MIT Press. — NBER nberch/10778; the patent thicket / complements problem.

Teece, D. J. (1986). Profiting from technological innovation: Implications for integration, collaboration, licensing and public policy. *Research Policy*, 15(6), 285–305. — DOI 10.1016/0048-7333(86)90027-2; appropriability regimes and complementary assets. The tollbooth mechanism in the innovation literature.

Tullock, G. (1967). The welfare costs of tariffs, monopolies, and theft. *Western Economic Journal*, 5(3), 224–232. — DOI 10.1111/j.1465-7295.1967.tb01923.x; rent-seeking as real resource loss.
